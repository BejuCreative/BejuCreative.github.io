How to turn a podcast into short clips

To turn a podcast into short clips, find moments that make sense without the rest of the episode, cut each one around a single idea, reframe it for a vertical screen, add clear captions and end on the point. Work through the whole recording, not just the first few minutes.

Pick moments that stand on their own

A clip has to work for someone who has never heard your show. Listen for three kinds of moment:

  • A complete answer. A question, a clear reply and a natural stop.
  • A story with an ending. The setup, the turn and the result in one run.
  • A clear opinion or a useful explanation that needs no earlier context.

Check how each candidate opens. If the first sentence starts with “that” or “he”, keep enough of the question to explain it, or choose another moment. Then write its takeaway in one sentence. If the takeaway needs the whole episode to explain, the moment needs more context or a different pick.

Keep the speaker’s meaning. Do not cut a qualification because a flat statement sounds punchier, especially when the talk is about health, money or any advice people act on.

How to turn a podcast into short clips, step by step

  1. Listen to the whole recording. Note every candidate with a start and an end time.
  2. Write one takeaway per candidate. Drop the weak ones. Five different ideas make a stronger set than five cuts of the same point.
  3. Trim each clip. Remove repeated setup, false starts and detours. Keep the small pauses that make the speaker sound natural.
  4. Reframe for vertical. Crop to 9:16 so the active speaker stays in view.
  5. Add captions. Check every name and technical term.
  6. End on the point. Export, then watch the clip as a viewer would.

Frame the clip for a vertical screen

A podcast is shot wide and a Short is tall. Crop toward whoever is speaking and keep their face in the upper half of the frame, clear of the captions and of the buttons a platform places along the bottom and the right edge. With two speakers, follow whoever talks, or stack both in one frame. If someone demonstrates something on screen, keep it in view, even when that means a looser crop on the face.

Caption for the viewer with the sound off

Write captions as if the sound is off, because for some viewers it is. Keep a few words on each line, use high contrast, and place the text in the lower middle of the frame, away from the platform’s controls. Highlight at most one key word at a time. Check guest names and specialist terms against your notes, not only against the transcript.

Open on the point and end on it

The first line decides whether someone stays. Start at the strongest sentence of the idea, not at the host’s introduction, and never promise something the clip does not deliver. End when the point has landed. Do not let the clip run on into the next question.

Review before you post

Watch each clip three times: once for meaning, once for how it looks, and once with the sound off to check the captions. Look at the first frame, the last frame and the spelling. Make sure the file is the version you approved.

Send your feedback in one message, with times. “At 0:08, remove the repeated sentence” is something an editor can act on. “Make it better” is not.

Hand it to an editor

If you would rather not cut the clips yourself, a short brief is enough:

  • Source: a link to the original recording, and any timestamps you already like.
  • Audience and platform: who the clip is for and where it will be posted.
  • Style: one or two examples you like, and your caption preferences.
  • Accuracy: guest names, specialist terms and statements that must stay unchanged.

Questions

How long should a podcast clip be?

There is no single right length. Cut each clip around one idea and end when the point has landed. Cut each clip around one idea, and end as soon as the point has landed. If a clip needs a second idea to make sense, it is really two clips.

How many clips can one episode give?

As many distinct ideas as the episode holds. Five different ideas make a stronger set than five cuts of the same point, so choose the number the recording can support.

Do I need the guest’s permission?

Check before you publish. Confirm that you have the right to repurpose the recording and that the guest is included in how you plan to use it.
