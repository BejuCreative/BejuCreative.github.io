How to turn a podcast into short clips

Start with one complete idea—not an arbitrary timestamp. Here is a practical workflow for repurposing an existing video episode.

By BEJÚ Creative · 1 October 2026

Get a free sample clipExplore podcast editing

1. Choose moments that stand alone

Listen for a specific answer, a useful explanation or a story with a clear ending. A clip should make sense to someone who has never seen your show. If an answer starts with “that” or “he”, either keep enough of the question to explain it or choose another moment.

Mark the start and end of each candidate and write a one-sentence takeaway. If you cannot describe the takeaway without explaining the whole episode, the moment probably needs more context. Five distinct ideas make a stronger brief than five cuts of essentially the same point.

Do not remove a qualification simply because a more absolute statement sounds punchier. Keep the speaker’s meaning intact, especially when the discussion includes health, money or other consequential advice.

2. Give the editor a usable brief

Share an original downloadable recording rather than a compressed screen recording where possible. Separate camera or audio tracks are useful if you have them; tell the editor how they align. Confirm that you have permission to repurpose the recording and include the guest in your planned use.

  • Source: footage link and any selected timestamps.
  • Audience: who should understand or benefit from the clip?
  • Destination: the account and platform where you will post.
  • Style: one or two examples, brand assets and caption preferences.
  • Accuracy: guest names, specialist terms and statements that must stay unchanged.
  • Scope: clip count, approximate length, export versions and deadline.

A useful brief might read: “Five clips from this interview for our business-owner audience. Keep the captions clean, preserve the guest’s terminology and finish each clip on its takeaway. These two examples show the pacing we like.”

3. Shape each clip around its point

The opening should tell a viewer why this moment is worth watching without promising something the clip does not deliver. Remove repeated setup and unnecessary detours, but keep pauses that help the speaker sound natural. Shorter is not automatically clearer.

For a vertical edit, check that the active speaker and any necessary demonstration stay visible. Captions should be readable and positioned away from interface controls on the intended platform. Confirm names and technical words against the brief rather than trusting an automatic transcription.

4. Review before you publish

Watch once for meaning, once for presentation and once with sound muted to check captions. Check the first frame, the ending, spelling, framing and any call to action. Make sure the final export is the version you approved.

Consolidate feedback into one message. “At 00:08, remove the repeated sentence” is actionable; “make it more viral” is not. Our standard short-form offer includes one revision round within the agreed brief. A new concept or substantially different brief should be discussed separately.

5. Keep a simple publishing record

Record the clip filename, post URL, account, posting date and the idea behind it. Compare results over the same time window rather than comparing a new clip with another clip’s lifetime views. Keep the comparison on the same platform, where the view definition is consistent.

How many clips should you order?

Choose the number that the episode can support with distinct, useful moments. A recording with two strong explanations may justify fewer clips than a longer interview with several complete stories. Do not buy more cuts just to meet a quota.
