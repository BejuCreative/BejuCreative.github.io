How video editing is priced and quoted

Video editing is priced by the amount and difficulty of the work: how many videos you need each month, how long they are, how much motion design goes into them, how clean the source material is, and whether revisions are included. A good quote lists all five, so two quotes can be compared like for like.

Ask what a video includes before you ask what it costs. Editing is custom work, so a price only means something next to a description of the job. This guide explains how video editing is priced, and what we look at when we quote it.

The five things that shape a quote

How many videos you need each month

Volume is the first number in any quote. A steady monthly schedule is planned differently from a single batch, because the studio can set aside time for it and learn your style as it goes.

How long the videos are

Length means two things. The finished video may be short while the source recording is long. Finding five good moments in a long interview is a different job from receiving five timestamp ranges that are already chosen. Tell the editor which job you are asking for.

How much motion design goes in

A clean talking-head edit with captions is a lighter job than a video with supporting visuals, animated titles or custom graphics. If your reference videos show motion design, say so up front, so it is part of the agreed edit and not a surprise.

The quality of the source material

Clear audio, steady cameras and original files make editing faster. Poor sound, several cameras that need syncing or heavily compressed files add work. Send the original recording whenever you can.

Whether revisions are included

Quotes differ on this, and some cap the number of feedback rounds. Ask what the revision terms are before you compare. At Beju Creative, revisions are unlimited on every edit, and we keep editing until you are happy.

Short-form, long-form and launch films are quoted differently

Short-form editing is quoted around a regular schedule of Shorts, Reels and TikToks cut from your long videos. Editing a full episode or a long video is a separate job with its own quote, and a short-form quote does not also cover an edited full episode. A SaaS or app launch film is quoted per project, because the product story, the motion work and the length all vary. A content system that we build and run for you is scoped around what you record and what you want to publish.

How to compare two quotes

Put the quotes side by side and check that they describe the same brief. Compare:

  • the number of finished videos in each billing period
  • the length of the source recordings and how the moments are chosen
  • the visual treatment: captions, graphics and motion design
  • the export versions: crops, languages and calls to action
  • the revision terms
  • how footage is supplied and when feedback is due

A quote that leaves out work you need is not comparable, however it is priced. A written scope also makes your own content calendar easier to plan. For interview footage, the guide to turning a podcast into short clips shows how to prepare a brief that makes the scope clear.
